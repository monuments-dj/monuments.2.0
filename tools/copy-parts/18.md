## Story section heading
- \ The problem
- Stop wagging the finger.

## Story stat
- 46%
- of Idaho grads went on after high school. One of the lowest rates in the country.

## Story body paragraphs
- A statewide campaign to lift Idaho's college-going rate by talking with teens instead of at them. I directed the experiential build that turned a hard conversation about the future into one 18,000+ teens chose to have.
- The usual fix is an adult telling a teen what they should do. The campaign had a name for those people: They Sayers. Nobody listens to a They Sayer.
- So Buck the Quo did the opposite. It talked to the teens themselves, not their parents, not their teachers, and treated them like the people actually making the decision. The brand had to feel like theirs before it could change a single mind.

## Activation section heading
- \ The activation · More In Store
- Meet them where they are.

## Activation subheading
- Most of this campaign happened in person. A mock-retail space that traveled Idaho, turned a heavy subject into a place teens chose to walk into, and earned a real conversation once they did.

## Scene 1
- 01 / The space
- A shipping container that pulled up like a store
- It traveled the state and parked where teens already were: fairs, events, the places adults usually skip. From the outside it read like a drop, not a lecture.
- SC-01 · The space

## Scene 2
- 02 / The hook
- A room built around one word
- FUTURE in neon, merch worth wearing, a saddle that said I AM, I CAN, I WILL. The space earned a few minutes of attention, then used them well.
- SC-02 · The hook

## Scene 3
- 03 / The turn
- The merch was the doorway, the conversation was the point
- Teens only, no parents, no teachers. A simple writing exercise opened a real talk about what they actually wanted their life to look like, with a near-peer who listened instead of advised.
- SC-03 · The turn

## My part body paragraph
- I started on Buck the Quo as part of Drake Cooper, then stayed with the campaign as a consultant as it grew. Over its run I worked nearly every side of it: behind the camera for the bulk of the photo and video, on the floor of the More In Store activation, in the room for the focus groups that kept it honest, and in the brand's replies across social. I directed the campaign's YouTube show.
